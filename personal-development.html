My Reading Reflection

Jump to My Biggest Takeaway

Reading The Richest Man in Babylon made me think on how important it is to be intentional with the choices I make, especially when it comes to money and my future. Oftentimes, it is mcuh easier to focus on what I want right in the moment without thinking about how my decisions will affect me later. The lessons in these chapters reminded me that building a better future starts with the small things I do today.

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

1. Which cure stood out to you, and why?

The cure that stood out to me the most was to start saving at least one-tenth of what I earn. I liked this lesson because it made me realise that saving is not only for people who earn a lot of money. It is about learning how to manage what I already have. Sometimes, we can spend money on little things without realising how quickly they add up. The book reminded me that even starting with a small amount is better than waiting until I have more money to begin saving. "Every mikkle mek a muckle" is a saying that comes to mind. I can start small and build the habit of saving, which will help me in the long run.

2. What does it mean in your current circumstances as a student?

As a student, who also works a full time job, at times, I find it difficult to balance my responsibilities while managing the various expenses I have to cover out my own pocket. Some of which include bills, transportation, tuition and other personal needs. And while, at the moment, my salary is not always enough to cover all my expenses, this lesson reminded me that I can still make an effort to save, even if it is a small amount. I can start by being more aware of how I spend my money and looking for ways to cut back on unnecessary expenses. This will then help me build a habit of saving and prepare me for future financial responsibilities, especially as I continue my studies and eventually enter the workforce as a professional software developer.

3. What specific habit could you start, change, or track?

One habit I have implemented since 2026, is the tracking of my expenses. I took the initiative to creating an excel sheet where I list my expected expense for each month according to my salary and how I hope to manage my finances. Thus far, it has been quite effective, however, I would also like to try adding another saving mechanism where I save all monies whenever I receive subsistance, travelling claims and or honorarium.

My goal is to become more disciplined with my money instead of only thinking about saving when I have "enough".

The Five Laws of Gold

1. Which law changed or challenged your thinking?

The law that challenged my thinking the most was the lesson that gold slips away from people who invest it in things they do not understand or in matters they are not familiar with. This stood out to me because it shows that wanting to make money is not enough. We also need to be careful about the decisions we make. Sometimes, an opportunity can sound good at first, but that does not mean it is the right choice to risk.

2. How would you recognise a sound financial opportunity?

I beleive that I would recognise a sound financial opportunity by taking the time to research from valid sites and sources, asking questions, and seeking advice from people who have experience in that area. I would want to understand how it works, what potential risks are there, and whether the person giving advice actually has experience in that area. One thing I took from the book is that advice should come from people who know what they are talking about. I would rather take my time and make an informed decision than rush into something and lose money because I did not understand it.

3. What is your biggest takeaway, and how will you apply it?

My biggest takeaway from the Five Laws of Gold is that protecting what I have is just as important as trying to get more. I used to think about money mainly in terms of having enough to cover what I need, but these lessons made me think more about planning ahead and making wiser choices. I can apply this by being careful with my spending, learning more about saving and investing, and seeking proper advice before making important financial decisions.

My Biggest Takeaway

Overall, reading these chapters helped me understand that personal development is not only about setting goals; it is also about building habits that help me reach them. The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse taught me to pay attention to what I earn, save consistently and control unnecessary spending. While The Five Laws of Gold taught me to protect my savings, be careful with financial opportunities and learn from people with the right knowledge and experience.

As a working class student, I know that I am still learning, and I may not be able to apply every lesson perfectly right away. However, I can start by being more aware of my spending, saving what I can and thinking more carefully before making decisions. Small steps can make a difference when I stay consistent. For me, the main lesson is that the way I handle small responsibilities today can help to prepare me for bigger responsibilities in the near future.
